Pressure unit converter

Type a pressure in any box and the other three update. Works for hPa, inHg, mmHg and kPa.

How the conversion works

Each unit is a fixed multiple of the others, so converting is one multiplication. The tool converts through hPa:

To getFrom hPa
inHghPa × 0.02953
mmHghPa × 0.75006
kPahPa ÷ 10

Going the other way, 1 inHg is 33.864 hPa, 1 mmHg is 1.3332 hPa and 1 kPa is 10 hPa. The tool keeps more digits than shown here.

Worked example

A weather report gives 30.13 inHg and you want hPa. Divide by 0.02953: about 1020.32 hPa. From there, 1020.32 hPa × 0.75006 is 765.30 mmHg, and 1020.32 hPa ÷ 10 is 102.032 kPa.

Limits

  • Results are rounded to 2 or 3 decimals. Your barometer is unlikely to be more exact than that.
  • inHg and mmHg are defined for a column of mercury under standard conditions. These factors are the standard ones, and they do not correct for the temperature of a real mercury barometer.
  • This page changes units only. It doesn't tell you what the weather will do. For that, see how to read a barometer.

Frequently asked

Is a millibar the same as a hectopascal? Yes. 1 mbar is exactly 1 hPa, so use the hPa box for a reading in millibars.

Is mmHg the same as torr? Almost. The two differ by less than one part in a million, far less than any barometer can show.

Can I convert a change in pressure as well as a reading? Yes. The factors are the same. A fall of 5 hPa is a fall of 0.15 inHg or 3.8 mmHg.

Does this convert station pressure to sea-level pressure? No. A converter changes the unit, not the place the pressure refers to. Use the sea-level pressure calculator for that.
